What you can do

Lowering exposure lowers what you breathe. Here are the steps with evidence behind them, at four levels, with honest labels on how much each one is known to help.

Labels on this page describe the evidence for each step. In pregnancy, ask your doctor or midwife before changing anything.

What the labels mean

You

Level 1
  • Check the air category and follow its advice. Official advice gets stricter as the category rises. [42,43,44] See the readings. Moderate
  • If you wear a mask, make sure it fits. In a laboratory test, leakage around worn masks ranged from 3% to 68%. [45] In trials in Beijing, masks improved short-term blood pressure and heart measures. [46,47] Moderate
  • Pregnant? We found no source on masks in pregnancy, so ask your doctor or midwife.

Your home

Level 2
  • Use a HEPA air cleaner sized for the room. It lowers indoor PM2.5 and slightly lowers blood pressure. [48,49,50] Moderate
  • In pregnancy, purifiers lower exposure, but the effect on birth weight is unclear. In Mongolia, purifiers cut indoor PM2.5 by 29%; the birth-weight difference was not statistically clear. [51,52] Limited
  • Cleaner cooking cuts smoke. Switching to gas (LPG) stoves cut smoke a lot, but babies were not significantly heavier, on average only 19.6 g. [53]
  • Keep windows closed during pollution peaks. [54] Limited
  • Don't rely on supplements. One tiny trial of B vitamins is not enough to advise them, and other scientists say the picture is incomplete. [23,55,56] In pregnancy, follow your clinician's advice on vitamins. Very limited

Community and workplace

Level 3
  • Protect the most exposed workers. Pakistani traffic workers and coal miners showed more DNA damage than comparison groups. [19,20] They may deserve priority in protection and research. Limited

Policy

Level 4
  • Emission controls work at scale. Between 2013 and 2017, China's population-weighted PM2.5 fell from 61.8 to 42.0 µg/m³, mostly because of emission controls. [57]
  • Homes matter in Punjab. About 23% of Punjab's PM2.5 comes from homes, mostly from solid-fuel cooking. [10]
  • Pakistan has a plan. The 2023 National Clean Air Policy lists measures such as cleaner fuels and preventing crop burning. [58]

Questions people ask

4 questions
I don't smoke. Can air pollution still cause lung cancer?

Yes, the evidence points that way. Never-smokers were included in the lung-cancer meta-analysis, and IARC researchers link about 195,000 adenocarcinomas worldwide in 2022 to outdoor particle pollution. [2,3]

Can a blood test show how much pollution has damaged my DNA?

No. DNA-damage, telomere and methylation tests are research tools for comparing groups of people. We found no official source supporting their use for individuals, so this initiative does not offer or recommend them. [59]

Should I take vitamins to protect against smog?

There is not enough evidence to recommend this. A small B-vitamin experiment showed changes in laboratory measures only, and other scientists say the picture is incomplete. [23,55]

Will a purifier protect my baby during pregnancy?

It lowers what you breathe at home. In a trial, purifiers cut indoor PM2.5 by 29%, but a clear improvement in birth weight was not shown. [51,52]
