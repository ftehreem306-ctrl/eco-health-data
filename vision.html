Our vision

From plain-language awareness now to ethical genomic and translational research that includes Pakistan's own data.

This page describes future aims, not current activities.

On this page

  2. What we do

The gap we see

Scientists use the word exposome for everything a person is exposed to over a lifetime, starting before birth, studied alongside the genome. [60] Large studies abroad already combine DNA and air-pollution data, including Sherlock-Lung, the PACE birth cohorts and UK Biobank. [16,22,24] Our review found no Pakistani study that has analysed air pollution alongside genomic data.

Pakistan already has major genetic resources. PROMIS has collected genetic and health data from thousands of people with heart attacks and comparison groups [61], and found many people carrying two non-working copies of certain genes. [62] The Pakistan Genome Resource has analysed exomes and genomes from 173,303 people. [63]

Pakistan's National Bioethics Committee issued national guidelines on human biological materials in 2020, but experts describe gaps in ethics, quality standards and data governance for biobanks. [64]

What we do

Translate

Turn research on air pollution, the genome, epigenetics and cancer into plain language for families, with every finding labelled by strength of evidence.

Open up the data

Publish referenced material, an open evidence table and daily air-quality readings that anyone can check and reuse.

Roadmap

  1. Now

    Plain-language awareness, open exposure data and an open evidence table.

  2. Next

    Partnerships with ethics-approved research groups, to bring Pakistani exposure data into genomic and epigenomic studies of cancer and pregnancy.

  3. Always

    Consent, data protection and community benefit first. This initiative will not collect samples or genetic data without ethical approval and appropriate governance.

Work with us
