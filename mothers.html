Mothers, babies and the next generation

Pollution reaches pregnancy in ways we can measure. What it does to the baby in the long run is still being studied.

What the labels mean

In one minute

  • KnownMore polluted air in pregnancy is linked to babies born early, small or stillborn. [4]
  • KnownSoot particles have been found in placentas, cord blood and fetal organs. [6,7]
  • SuspectedNewborns of more exposed mothers show small differences in DNA tags. [24]
  • UnknownEffects on grandchildren, or through fathers' sperm: no source found.

Birth outcomes

2 findings

Known Early and small births

A US review covered 68 studies and about 32.8 million births. Of the 58 studies on air pollutants, 48 (84%) found significant links with preterm birth, low birth weight or stillbirth. [4] An earlier meta-analysis found small but significant associations with preterm birth and low birth weight. [37]

Suspected The global picture (modelled)

In 2019, outdoor and indoor PM2.5 together were estimated to account for 15.6% of low-birth-weight births and 35.7% of preterm births worldwide: about 2.76 million babies born too small and 5.87 million born too early. South Asia, which includes Pakistan, had the highest burden of any region. [5]

Particles reach the unborn baby

2 findings

Known Present in the placenta and fetal organs

Soot (black carbon) particles were found in human placentas, and the amount was linked to the soot level where the mother lived. [6] Experts later debated exactly which side of the placenta the particles were on. [38,39]

In a study of 60 mothers and their newborns, soot was found in every sample of maternal blood, placenta and cord blood, and in fetal liver, lung and brain tissue. [7]

Unknown What the particles do

What these particles do to the baby is not yet known. [7]

Newborn DNA

2 findings

Suspected Small differences at birth

Babies whose mothers breathed more PM2.5 showed small methylation differences at 14 DNA sites, in a meta-analysis of 9 birth cohorts. Whether this affects their health is unknown. [24]

In Belgium, each 5 µg/m³ more PM2.5 during pregnancy was associated with telomeres 8.8% shorter in cord blood and 13.2% shorter in the placenta. [40] In 1,396 European children, the estimate for PM2.5 during pregnancy was not statistically significant. [41]

Unknown Grandchildren and fathers

Our review found no source showing that air pollution affects grandchildren through inherited epigenetic changes, and no approved source on fathers' exposure and sperm. We make no claim about either.
